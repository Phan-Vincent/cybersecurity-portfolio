### What We Are Doing

Upon discovering this incident, we immediately:

1. **Secured our systems** — We isolated affected systems, changed all administrative passwords, and engaged a leading cybersecurity firm to conduct a forensic investigation.
2. **Investigated the scope** — We worked with cybersecurity experts to determine what information may have been accessed and which patients were affected.
3. **Notified authorities** — We notified the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Office for Civil Rights (OCR).
4. **Reviewed and strengthened security** — We are enhancing our security measures, including implementing additional endpoint detection, improving network segmentation, and conducting staff security awareness training.
5. **Offered protections** — See "What You Can Do" below for services we are making available.

### What You Can Do

We encourage you to take the following steps to protect your information:

- **Monitor your health insurance statements** for any services you did not receive. If you see anything suspicious, contact your insurer immediately.
- **Review your credit reports** for any unauthorized accounts or inquiries. You can obtain free credit reports from each of the three major credit reporting agencies at **www.annualcreditreport.com** or by calling **1-877-322-8228**.
- **Consider placing a fraud alert or credit freeze** on your credit file by contacting one of the three major credit bureaus:
  - **Equifax:** 1-800-525-6285 or **www.equifax.com**
  - **Experian:** 1-888-397-3742 or **www.experian.com**
  - **TransUnion:** 1-800-680-7289 or **www.transunion.com**
- **Report any suspected identity theft** to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) at **www.identitytheft.gov** or by calling **1-877-438-4338**.
